How the course works

Three stages. No surprises.

  1. Ground school · 40 hours

    About 3 months part-time in Singapore: aerodynamics, navigation, weather and aviation law, taught step by step.

  2. Simulator training · 20 hours

    Hands-on, safe practice on realistic flight simulators before real aircraft.

  3. Flight phase · Australia

    18 days in Australia: 15 hrs dual and 5 hrs solo flight training, 5 extra flying hours and a 1-hour flight test with the Chief Flying Instructor, leading to the RAAus certificate.

Is my teen eligible?

Ages 15 and above

No Class 2 or Class 3 medical certificate is needed for the RPC. We’ll walk through any age and health questions in your consultation.

Common questions

Everything parents ask us

What is the Recreational Pilot Course (RPC)?

A structured introduction to flying for ages 15+, combining ground school, simulator training and a flight phase in Australia.

Is it safe for my teen?

Training is dual-instructed, and your teen only flies solo after instruction. Aeroviation partners with a leading Australian flight school, with yearly school visits, and the course ends with a flight test with the Chief Flying Instructor.

How much does it cost, and can I pay in instalments?

The all-inclusive package is SGD 18,911 including 9% GST, with in-house loan and flexible payment terms available. It covers return flights, 4-star hotel, daily transport in Australia, travel & medical insurance, uniform, learning materials and RAAus fees. Daily expenses, meals and leisure are not included.

What happens in Australia?

18 days in Australia with 15 hrs dual and 5 hrs solo flight training, plus 5 extra flying hours and the flight test. Return flights, hotel, tourist visa for Singaporeans and airport transfers are included.

Does my teen need a medical or any experience?

No prior experience is needed, and the RPC doesn’t require a Class 2 or 3 medical certificate. Teens can start ground school and simulator training from 13, fly in aircraft from 14 and earn the RPC at 15.

What comes after the RPC?

All RPC hours are credited. The RPC can convert to a GA Recreational Pilot Licence, with the route onward to a PPL or CPL, and counts towards the 40 hours needed for a PPL.
